01 / SUNLIGHT ZONE / 20 M

Life on the reef

Clownfish

Near the surface, sunlight fills the reef with colour. Clownfish live among the tentacles of sea anemones, which give them shelter from many predators. A protective mucus coating helps them live safely in this unusual home. Our journey begins in warm, shallow water.

02 / TWILIGHT ZONE / 400 M

The light begins to fade

Chambered nautilus

As we go deeper, the water becomes darker. Along deep reef slopes, a chambered nautilus searches for food with its many tentacles. Chambers inside its spiral shell help control buoyancy. Usually found around 200 to 500 metres deep, it lives near the edge of the sunlit world.

03 / MIDNIGHT ZONE / 1,500 M

A world without sunlight

Fangtooth

Sunlight no longer reaches this depth. The fangtooth has large teeth that help it catch and hold on to fish and crustaceans. Despite its fierce appearance, it grows to only about 18 centimetres. In this dark world, finding the next meal is a challenge.

04 / ABYSSAL ZONE / 4,000 M

Floating through the abyss

Dumbo octopus

In cold, dark water, a dumbo octopus moves with gentle beats of its ear-like fins. Its webbed arms open like a small umbrella as it swims above the seafloor. Different species live at different depths, including the abyss, where they feed on small animals.

05 / HADAL ZONE / 7,500 M

Life in the trenches

Mariana snailfish

In the Mariana Trench, a pale snailfish lives under enormous pressure. Scientists have collected this species from roughly 6,900 to 8,000 metres deep. It feeds on small crustaceans near the trench floor. Even here, far below the waves, the ocean is full of life.
